Technicians in pilot reviews paused between AI diagnosis and parts ordering. Product HP Field Service

HP field technicians diagnose devices on site, talk with customers, and order parts under SLA pressure. The mobile experience spans unstable connectivity, parts accuracy, and AI suggestions the technician must be able to explain on the truck.

Problem The gap between diagnosis and commit

Technicians saw a ranked list of AI suggestions and then hesitated. They had options. They did not have one next step they could explain to the customer or commit to before ordering.

Approach One path they can trust

I mapped the diagnosis-to-parts journey and designed one clear recommendation with explicit alternates, plain-language rationale on every suggestion, and override that logs without punishing. Diagnosis context carries into parts without re-entry. Live, partial, and offline states are first-class so technicians know what is safe to act on.

Outcome Diagnosis to parts in one thread

Technicians could state why a part was suggested and change it without losing context. That removed the hesitation we saw when AI output and parts flow lived in separate mental models.

Evidence

I led the design end to end with field service ops, parts dispatch, and engineering in the room. Mapped the diagnosis-to-parts journey, designed AI state and override patterns in Figma, and validated flows with technicians in three operating contexts.

Design lens

Consistency and standards

AI states, override paths, and offline behavior had to use patterns engineering had already built. I used the existing React Native design system, not throwaway research frames.

Figma flows built on the component library that shipped.

Insights: User testing

Three operating contexts. One set of patterns for each.

Evidence

Twelve sessions. Every insight maps to a screen state, not a survey row.

I ran three context types: full signal, uncertain diagnosis, and offline. Each had its own failure mode. The patterns had to work across all three.

  • Technicians want sequence over volume: what to verify first, not longer ranked lists.
  • The gap between seeing a likely diagnosis and being willing to commit to a parts order matters more than raw model scores on the truck.
  • Override and recovery paths must feel safe, not punitive.

Design lens

Help prevent mistakes

When parts confidence is uncertain, the next action UI must say so. Technicians stopped second-guessing when the uncertainty state was labeled, not hidden.

Ambiguous-state sessions showed the most variance before the partial-confidence indicator was added.

Five options and I don't know which to trust → Top recommended part with plain rationale, alternates one tap away, override without a warning screen.

Recommendations: Recommendations and implementation guide

Agency beats cleverness on the truck.

Evidence

One recommended part with explicit alternates, rationale visible on every suggestion, and override that logs without punishing.

Ship with AI state patterns (live, partial, offline), scan-and-confirm for parts identity so the technician closes the loop on physical reality, and diagnosis context carried into parts without re-entry.

Implementation guide

  • AI states: live, partial, and offline each get a distinct UI treatment. No spinners as the only failure state.
  • Override: logged and visible in the record, never shown as an error or warning to the technician.

Recommendations

  • Default to one recommended part with explicit alternates tied to verification steps. Show the rationale in plain language beside the recommendation.
  • Use scan-and-confirm for parts identity so the technician closes the loop on physical reality before the order commits.

Design lens

Recognition rather than recall

Ranked list forced technicians to recall which option was safer. One recommended part with a plain reason lets them read the decision, not reconstruct it.

Pilot replay: parts commit time dropped when rationale was visible beside the recommendation.

Five options and I don't know which to choose → Top part with reason shown, alternates below, override one tap away without a penalty state.
