How do we make earn-and-spend legible before scaling the rewards marketplace?

Attendance and time-on-platform were falling while points piled up with nowhere to go. I ran a foundational study—problem frame, design-system stimulus, and moderated testing—to define one earn-and-spend story product could ship before marketplace UI scaled.

Problem Loop not legible

Learners earned points and creator coins but could not describe how to spend them. Attendance and session time dropped while the product prepared to scale marketplace and avatar UI—without a validated earn-and-spend story in the journey.

Approach Design practice

I framed the retention question with product, built moderated stimulus on the design system (not throwaway comps), and paired every verbatim to the frame on screen. Engineering reviewed the same components before recommendations became a ship plan.

Outcome Shippable foundation

One ledger, onboarding before the shop, and aspiration-led marketplace framing—staged so ledger clarity shipped first. Post-launch: 43% higher attendance and 27% higher course completion in the weeks from the milestone.

Objective

Validate the earn-and-spend mental model before UI scales.

Evidence

Product was ready to invest in marketplace and avatar work.

The design practice goal was simple: agree what learners should see in wallet, onboarding, and marketplace surfaces—then scale UI with engineering confidence, not parallel currency experiments.

Design lens

Match between system and real world

Children talked in heroes, powers, and class wins—not ledger types the product never taught.

DS-aligned wallet, avatar grid, and redeem paths used in all 15 moderated sessions.

Problem

Retention slipped when the rewards loop was hard to read.

Evidence

Points accumulated; attendance and time-on-platform fell.

Loyalty mechanics sat outside the core class journey. Dual currency and missing affordances meant learners could not name their next spend action.

  • I think 1 coin = 2 points because coins are more precious than points
  • Points? nothing, i just have them, i dont know what to do with them

Design lens

Visibility of system status

Balances were visible; the path from balance to redeem was not—so intent stalled.

Synthesis linked platform decline to a missing earn-and-spend loop in the product story.

Insights: User testing

Evidence stays on the artefact.

Evidence

Fifteen sessions; every theme maps to a frame learners touched.

HCI signal and UI evidence stay paired—what they said is what they pointed at. That discipline kept synthesis honest when product asked what to build first.

  • Learners invented conversion rules because the product showed two currencies with no single balance story.
  • Creator coins felt random—children guessed at triggers instead of reading clear earn rules on screen.
  • Redemption intent was real; the marketplace surface did not show a obvious spend path from balance to hero.

Recommendations: Recommendations and implementation guide

Decisions engineering could schedule.

Evidence

One ledger, onboarding before browse, heroes over currency jargon.

Recommendations were ordered for ship: ledger and onboarding first, then marketplace depth—tokens, UX writing, in-product comms, and a single earn → balance → redeem journey.

Implementation guide

  • Ledger: one spendable balance, spent history, visible next action on marketplace.
  • Onboarding: earn and spend modules at deploy, before shop exposure.

Recommendations

  • One ledger: a single spendable balance, plain-language labels, and visible spent history on the marketplace surface.
  • Onboarding at deploy: short earn and spend modules before learners enter the shop—not after confusion shows up in support.

Design lens

Recognition rather than recall

Single ledger plus plain-language onboarding before marketplace browse.
