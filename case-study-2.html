HP: AI-assisted UX for field service

The design goal was straightforward: move technicians from symptom to defensible action faster, with parts flow, dispatch logic, and customer trust aligned in one visit.

⏱ Pilot-to-delivery enterprise engagement

🧭 Senior UX consultant (Birlasoft × HP)

🤝 Service leadership, delivery, engineering, field pilots

🛠 Figma, React, service tooling, analytics instrumentation

Context, business north star, and constraints

Context

HP’s field ecosystem spans on-site diagnosis, customer conversation, and parts workflows—uneven connectivity, variable device history, and SLAs that reward first-time resolution. Leadership and delivery invested in joint design reviews and pilot-ready flows, not a disposable concept deck.

My role: Senior UX consultant (Birlasoft × HP)—journey-to-screen translation on the highest-friction splits: diagnosis, AI-assisted recommendation, and ordering; facilitation with service, engineering, and pilot technicians.

Business north star

North star: Less time to a confident, explainable next action on site.

Success was measured in technician commitment and handoff quality—not model cleverness or list length.

Constraints

  • Thin and uneven connectivity — offline-tolerant patterns; clear stale vs live state.
  • Parts and trust — mis-ordered parts burn money, time, and customer confidence.
  • Explainability — agents must defend choices to customers and dispatch; “black box” erodes adoption.
  • Client metrics — detailed turnaround and FTR numbers remain confidential; outcomes below are process-observed and directional.

Operating contexts

Three field modes we designed against (no separate tabs—same vertical spine).

  • Stable visit — strong signals: shorten path to bounded checks, then parts with minimal navigation noise.
  • Ambiguous fault — model uncertainty explicit; safe ordering of probes; override one gesture away.
  • Thin connectivity — cache vs defer; UI prevents acting on ghost state.

User voice, data insight, and methodology

User voice

In interviews and walkthroughs, technicians privileged sequence over volume: what to verify first, what rules a hypothesis out, and what triggers a parts commit. Confidence meant an audit trail—not marketing tone.

Stakeholder investment: HP service and delivery funded joint reviews and pilots; roles per methodology stage are in the expand rows below.

Data insight

What the evidence pointed to

Agents were rarely blocked by a lack of data. They were blocked by commit latency: the gap between seeing signals and taking an action they could explain to a customer, dispatch, and their own team. AI that only adds ranked possibilities widens that gap; AI framed as inspectable next steps narrows it.

Triangulation: contextual inquiry themes + moderated flows showed the same gap between “options shown” and “order placed.”

Insights and recommendations

Insights

Interpretation: the work aligned teams on next-best-action language and reduced conceptual distance between “what we think is wrong” and “what we ship to the truck.”

Caveats: HP client performance metrics are not published here; cards separate workshop-observed process signals from directional product intent (G1–G2 style claims until telemetry ships).

Observed (process)

  • Shared language for next-best-action across diagnostic and ordering critiques.
  • Design reviews consistently tested override, partial data, and recovery—not only happy-path AI.

Directional (product intent)

  • Fewer conceptual handoffs between diagnosis narrative and parts execution.
  • Stronger path to measure turnaround time and first-time resolution once client metrics are publishable.

Reusable principle

Agency beats cleverness: AI UX that respects the person who owns the wrench.

Adoption in operations is a trust contract—the interface should make expert judgment easier, not stage a contest between human and model.

Recommendations and key decisions

Trade-offs stated explicitly—constraint, tension, call.

Next-best-action over long recommendation lists

Constraint: ranked lists increased scan time without increasing commitment—agents still had to build their own plan.

Decision: Default UI promoted a single primary path with explicit alternates, each tied to a verification step—not an open-ended pick list.

Scan-and-confirm for parts identity

Constraint: mis-ordered parts are expensive in money, time, and customer trust.

Decision: Part identification flows used scan-plus-confirm patterns so the agent—not the system—closed the loop on physical reality.

Rationale visible for trust and override

Constraint: “black box” suggestions erode adoption; agents need to defend choices on site.

Decision: Surfaced why this suggestion in operator language, with clear disagree / adjust paths that did not punish the user for model error.

Aligned diagnosis → ordering handoff

Constraint: re-entry across tools recreated the same delay the AI was supposed to remove.

Decision: Reduced handoff steps so diagnostic context carried into parts actions without duplicate data entry.
