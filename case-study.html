Case study 01

Stryker: UX Systems for Robotic Surgery Operations

Designing compliance-aware backend workflows and decision-support touchpoints for high-stakes healthcare operations.

Business goal Reduce workflow risk in clinical operations
User goal Improve clarity for time-critical decisions
Design focus Reliable, compliant, edge-case-first UX

Context

Where this work sat

At Birlasoft, I worked on Stryker's healthcare operations workflows that support robotic-surgery-related delivery and coordination. The system touched critical backend interactions where delays, missed notifications, or unclear state transitions could cascade into operational risk.

Role

My scope

I led UX research and service workflow design across high-risk operational journeys, partnering with product and engineering teams to define decision points, notification logic, and interface behavior under compliance constraints.

Problem

How do you design backend operations UX so teams can move fast without compromising safety, compliance, or clinical confidence?

Constraints

  • Regulatory and compliance requirements (including GDPR-sensitive data handling).
  • Time-critical notifications where delays or misses created downstream risk.
  • Complex multi-actor workflows across operations and care teams.
  • Edge-case-heavy scenarios that were rare but high impact.

Approach

  • Mapped high-risk journeys and failure states before moving into UI refinements.
  • Defined interaction patterns for alerts, escalations, and state visibility.
  • Prioritized actions by risk severity and operational urgency.
  • Validated decisions with cross-functional teams to balance speed and reliability.

Key decisions

Design choices made to reduce ambiguity in critical workflows.

01

Alert clarity

Structured notifications around urgency and required next action, not just status updates.

02

Action hierarchy

Made high-risk tasks visually and interactionally prominent to reduce missed critical steps.

03

Traceability

Designed clearer system state and handoff visibility to improve accountability across teams.

04

Edge-case readiness

Built patterns for exceptions and fallback actions when normal flow assumptions failed.

Solution

A resilient operations UX layer for complex healthcare delivery.

  • Redesigned backend workflows to make task status and ownership explicit.
  • Introduced risk-aware notification and escalation interaction patterns.
  • Aligned information architecture with compliance and auditability needs.
  • Created reusable decision-support UX patterns for future modules.

Outcomes

Operational confidence improved through better decision visibility and safer interaction design.

Risk handling Critical actions surfaced with greater clarity
Workflow reliability Reduced ambiguity in handoffs and escalations
Scalability Reusable patterns for future healthcare modules

What I learned

In healthcare operations, great UX is not about simplification alone. It is about helping teams make the right decision quickly, clearly, and safely under real-world constraints.
