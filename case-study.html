Case File 01 · Healthcare Operations UX

Healthcare operations: safer decisions at operational speed

I redesigned robotic-surgery operations UX around next-step clarity, explicit ownership, and escalation logic so clinical teams could move quickly without compromising compliance rigor.

⏱ 12-month enterprise program

🧭 UX strategy lead for operations workflows

🤝 Product, clinical ops, engineering, compliance

🛠 Figma, React, React Native, Model N, MongoDB

CTContext

Operational ambiguity created avoidable risk and delay.

The organization operated in a high-acuity environment where small UX ambiguities created handoff delay, rework, and avoidable risk.

The mandate was enterprise optimization: establish one decision-support language for risk, ownership, and escalation across modules.

My role: I led UX strategy for operations workflows, including failure-state mapping, alert hierarchy, mode design, and language alignment with engineering and compliance.

The north star prioritized safe speed, not interface density.

North star: enable safer clinical decisions at operational speed, with explicit handoffs when risk rises.

The program prioritized correct action under pressure over screen-level optimization.

Constraints demanded traceability, shared ownership, and legacy fit.

  • Regulatory accountability: each critical state change had to remain auditable without slowing incident response.
  • Distributed ownership: surgeons, robotics staff, and hospital operations each held partial context.
  • Legacy integration: new patterns had to fit existing modules and enterprise release cadence.
  • Data confidentiality: client metrics are private, so outcomes are reported as observed and directional.

Sign-off status: Pending review from Product Owner, UX Designer, and Developer.

OBObservations

Teams asked for next action and owner, not more status.

Across sessions, operators described the same failure mode: urgency without a clear action ladder, and handoffs that disappeared into chat threads.

Stakeholder investment: sponsors opened real workflows; engineering funded iteration; compliance joined early—roles per step are spelled out in methodology below.

Evidence showed action clarity mattered more than data volume.

What the evidence pointed to

The organization had optimized for information completeness in operations surfaces. Frontline operators were drowning in status that looked equally important—and they kept asking for one thing: what to do next, right now, with ownership visible when something crossed a risk threshold.

Triangulation: workshop and shadowing notes repeatedly surfaced “next action + owner,” not more tiles or feeds—consistent with the quote pattern above.

A six-step method translated risk findings into reusable delivery patterns.

I used a six-step method that moved from observed risk to reusable delivery patterns.

01 · Map failure states

Captured where decision ambiguity created delay, rework, or unsafe handoffs.

02 · Define risk-action language

Aligned operations, product, and compliance on one escalation ladder and ownership verbs.

03 · Prototype operating modes

Built interface states for steady and urgent conditions so next action remained obvious.

04 · Run scenario validation

Tested realistic incident flows with cross-functional teams to expose copy and state gaps.

05 · Validate traceability

Confirmed each critical transition answered audit and accountability requirements.

06 · Ship reusable patterns

Delivered a repeatable interaction grammar so future modules inherited the same risk logic.

IDInsights and decisions

The work established a shared risk-priority language for critical operations, reduced review friction, and improved confidence in future module rollouts.

INInsights

  • A shared risk-priority model aligned stakeholders on critical alerts.
  • Reviews converged faster with consistent state and ownership vocabulary.
  • Escalation pathways became easier to follow during walkthroughs.

DEDecisions

  • Prioritized alerts by risk tier and required next action over event chronology.
  • Elevated high-risk tasks and removed non-essential controls in urgent states.
  • Introduced explicit ownership states and handoff checkpoints across teams.

Reusable principle

In high-risk operations, clarity improves speed, especially during handoffs.

In the next cycle, I would align paired metrics earlier with compliance, including time to next action and handoff completion, so directional outcomes convert into publishable quantified claims.
